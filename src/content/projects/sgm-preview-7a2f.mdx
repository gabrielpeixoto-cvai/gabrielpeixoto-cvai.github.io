## Abstract

Generative motion policies, from diffusion planners to vision-language-action models, learn complex manipulation from demonstrations. However, they have no mechanism to avoid collisions with a previously unseen or moving obstacle. We present a training-free safety layer that uses a single control barrier function specification to protect the whole arm of a frozen generative policy from collisions. The specification is enforced as soft guidance at generation time, as a model predictive control filter at execution time, or as both. The layer anticipates obstacle motion and reroutes every arm link and the carried object around the obstacle, instead of restricting the constraint to the end effector or braking along the motion plan. The layer is generator-agnostic and embodiment-agnostic by construction. We evaluate it on diffusion and flow-matching policies, across two manipulation tasks, four simulated robot arms and a real robot, with a human arm model as the moving obstacle. The filter is the strongest configuration, raising the safe success rate from 20.5% for the frozen policy and 32.7% for the best baseline to 61.5% with no collision. The same layer lowers the collision rate of two frozen vision-language-action policies without retraining. To the best of our knowledge this is the first training-free safety layer that reroutes the whole arm of a frozen generative policy in anticipation of a moving obstacle, as in shared human-robot workspaces.

## Contributions

- **The safety layer.** We integrate capsule distances, control barrier functions, obstacle motion prediction and model predictive control into one training-free safety layer that protects the whole arm of a frozen generative policy.
- **An evaluation protocol.** The evaluation runs in three tiers. Isolation experiments test the layer without a generator, in-task experiments add it to the generator on two manipulation tasks and two VLA policies, and deployment experiments use estimated obstacle geometry and a real robot.
- **Where safety must be enforced.** Only the execution loop acts at every control tick and can correct the robot between two motion plans. On the pick-and-place task, the filter raises the safe success rate from 20.5 % for the frozen policy and 32.7 % for the best baseline to 61.5 % with no collision.

## Method

The safety layer protects the whole arm of a robot driven by a frozen generative policy from static and moving obstacles by a positive margin. It approximates the robot and the obstacles by capsules, whose pairwise distances are available in closed form. One CBF condition is defined on these distances. We present three configurations that enforce it: guidance at generation time, a predictive safety filter at execution time, and both in sequence, which we call the hybrid. Generation time is when the generative model forms the motion plan, and execution time is when the robot moves under closed-loop control. This section defines the geometry and the barrier first, then the filter, the guidance, and the hybrid, and closes with the interface that connects the filter to each generator.

#### Capsules

We approximate the robot and every obstacle by capsules. A capsule is a line segment with a radius. Obstacle capsule $m$ has endpoints $o^{(1)}_m$ and $o^{(2)}_m$ and radius $r_m$, and robot capsule $l$ has endpoints $p^{(1)}_l(q)$ and $p^{(2)}_l(q)$ and radius $r_l$. Points on the two segments are

$$
a(s) = p^{(1)}_l + s\,\bigl(p^{(2)}_l - p^{(1)}_l\bigr), \qquad b(t) = o^{(1)}_m + t\,\bigl(o^{(2)}_m - o^{(1)}_m\bigr), \qquad s, t \in [0, 1].
$$

The closest pair $(s^*, t^*)$ minimizes $\lVert a(s) - b(t) \rVert$, and the signed distance between the two capsules is

$$
d_{lm}(q) = \lVert a(s^*) - b(t^*) \rVert - (r_l + r_m),
$$

which is negative when the capsules overlap.

#### Barrier

We define one barrier for each pair of a robot capsule and an obstacle capsule, as its signed distance reduced by the margin $r_{\mathrm{safe}}$,

$$
h_{lm}(q) = d_{lm}(q) - r_{\mathrm{safe}}.
$$

The safe set $\mathcal{C}$ is the set of configurations at which every barrier is nonnegative,

$$
\mathcal{C} = \bigcap_{l,m}\{\,q : h_{lm}(q) \ge 0\,\}.
$$

The layer commands a joint velocity $\dot q_k$ at control tick $k$ and holds it for one control period $\Delta t$, so the next configuration is

$$
q_{k+1} = q_k + \dot q_k\,\Delta t.
$$

Each barrier has relative degree one, so a condition on the next tick is sufficient, and we impose the discrete-time CBF condition on every pair,

$$
h_{lm}(q_{k+1}) \ge (1-\gamma)\,h_{lm}(q_k),
$$

where the barrier gain $\gamma$, with $0<\gamma\le 1$, is the largest fraction of the current barrier value that one tick may remove.

#### Predictive filter

The filter acts at execution time and corrects each command before the robot executes it. It expresses the first $N$ actions of the motion plan as joint increments $\Delta q_0,\dots,\Delta q_{N-1}$, where $N$ is the horizon, and at every tick adds them to the measured configuration $q_0$,

$$
q^{\mathrm{ref}}_0 = q_0, \qquad q^{\mathrm{ref}}_k = q_0 + \sum_{j<k} \Delta q_j, \qquad k = 1,\dots,N.
$$

It chooses the joint velocities $\dot q_0,\dots,\dot q_{N-1}$, which move the robot through the integrator above, and minimizes a cost that keeps the configurations close to the reference and penalizes large velocities,

$$
J = \sum_{k=1}^{N} w_{\mathrm{track}}\,\lVert q_k - q^{\mathrm{ref}}_k\rVert^2 + \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} w_{\mathrm{eff}}\,\lVert \dot q_k\rVert^2,
$$

with weights $w_{\mathrm{track}}$ and $w_{\mathrm{eff}}$, subject to the CBF condition at every step. The obstacle may move during the horizon, so each obstacle endpoint keeps its current velocity $V^{(i)}_m$,

$$
o^{(i)}_{m,k} = o^{(i)}_m + V^{(i)}_m\,k\,\Delta t, \qquad i = 1, 2.
$$

When no command satisfies every condition, each CBF condition is relaxed with a nonnegative slack $s$, penalized by

$$
\rho \sum s^2 + \nu \sum s,
$$

with weights $\rho,\nu>0$ summed over every relaxed condition.

#### Guidance

Guidance acts at generation time. It adds to the clean prediction the gradient of a repulsion potential on the capsule distances of every pair,

$$
U = \sum_{k=1}^{H}\sum_{l,m} \exp\bigl(-\alpha\,h_{lm}(q_k)\bigr),
$$

where $q_k$ is the configuration at step $k$ of the clean prediction, $H$ is the number of steps of the motion plan, and the sharpness $\alpha>0$ sets the range of the repulsion. Diffusion Policy and the diffusion planner predict the clean motion plan $\hat x_0$ directly, and guidance updates it as

$$
\hat x_0 \leftarrow \hat x_0 - s\,w_i\,P\,\nabla U,
$$

where $s$ is the guidance scale, $w_i$ the weight of sampling step $i$, and $P$ a proximity factor that grows as the closest pair approaches. Flow matching predicts a velocity $v$ from the noisy sample $x_\tau$, and guidance updates the clean prediction with the sampling time $\tau$,

$$
\hat x_0 = x_\tau - \tau\,v, \qquad \hat x_0 \leftarrow \hat x_0 - s\,w_i\,P\,\tau\,\nabla U.
$$

#### Hybrid

The hybrid applies both configurations in sequence. Guidance shapes the motion plan at generation time, and the filter then takes the guided motion plan as its reference and imposes the CBF condition at every tick.

#### End-effector wrapper

The filter works on joint configurations, while each generator produces actions in the space in which it was trained, so an interface converts each motion plan into the joint increments of the reference. Joint actions convert exactly: increments are used directly, and absolute targets $q^{\mathrm{tgt}}_k$ are differenced against the configuration at the replanning step,

$$
\Delta q_0 = q^{\mathrm{tgt}}_0 - q_0, \qquad \Delta q_k = q^{\mathrm{tgt}}_k - q^{\mathrm{tgt}}_{k-1}, \quad k \ge 1.
$$

Some generators, including the two VLA policies, output end-effector displacements. The displacement $a_k$ becomes a twist $\xi_k = c_{\mathrm{cmd}}\,a_k$, where $c_{\mathrm{cmd}}$ converts command units to metres, and the damped least-squares inverse of the Jacobian $J$ maps the twist to a joint increment,

$$
\Delta q_k = \operatorname{clip}\bigl(J^{\top}(JJ^{\top}+\lambda^2 I)^{-1}\xi_k,\ \pm\Delta q_{\max}\bigr),
$$

where the damping $\lambda>0$ keeps the increment bounded near singular configurations and $\Delta q_{\max}$ bounds it.

### Setup

The experiments answer the following questions.

- **Q1.** Is protecting the end effector enough, or must the whole arm be protected?
- **Q2.** In dynamic scenarios, does predicting the obstacle motion help, compared with reactive safety?
- **Q3.** Should safety be enforced at generation time, at execution time, or both?
- **Q4.** How much task success does safety cost?
- **Q5.** Which components give our filter its advantage?
- **Q6.** When does the filter stop working, and does the limit come from the robot kinematics or from the formulation?
- **Q7.** Is the layer generic, or tailored to one robot, task or policy?
- **Q8.** Can the layer be deployed under real-world conditions?

We evaluate every method with the metrics below.

- **Safe success rate (Safe-Succ., %):** the percentage of episodes that complete the task without any collision with the obstacle. It is the primary metric, because a policy can be safe by failing the task, so task success and safety are counted together.
- **Task success rate (Success, %):** the percentage of episodes that complete the task.
- **Collision rate (Coll., %):** the percentage of episodes in which the robot or the object it holds overlaps the obstacle. It is measured between the collision meshes of the simulator, not between the capsules of the layer, so the margin of the capsules cannot hide a contact.
- **Maximum slack (Max-slk, mm):** the largest slack that the soft barrier rows need at any step of the horizon. It shows how far the filter falls short of the barrier condition.
- **Peak deviation (Dev-pk, mm):** the largest distance of the end effector from the motion plan, until the task succeeds or the episode ends, taken as the median over episodes. The generator replans from the configuration that the robot reached, so the distance at each tick is measured to the motion plan in force at that tick.
- **Control rate (Ctrl, Hz):** the number of control ticks per second, averaged over episodes that run one at a time, measured end to end.

In the isolation experiments, each collision rate carries ± the larger distance to the bounds of its 95% Wilson interval.

The baselines run as their papers or released code describe them, with the adaptations that our task needs. Each one runs on our generators with ten sampling steps, against the moving human arm, which it reads again at each motion plan or control tick. PACS runs its separation-monitoring variant, which its paper presents as its own, because our code does not model contact energy. Our reproduction of AEGIS skips its barrier while the obstacle is more than 0.50 m from the end effector, a gate that AEGIS does not have. When the solver of a baseline finds no solution, the generated command passes unchanged. The published code contains every setting.

#### Evaluation protocol

- **Seeds.** Every obstacle scene of a task runs with the seeds 0 to 9 of the task scene, or 0 to 49 in the convex-obstacle task experiment. On the Panda robot, the seed controls a joint noise of 0.02 rad on the start configuration and the position and rotation of the can in its box. On the other arms, the seed controls only the can. Every method runs from the same start state of each seed. We exclude a seed when, after the reset and before the first action, the robot or the payload overlaps the obstacle, and the excluded seed leaves every method of its table. The robot is scored on every step of a kept episode, and the payload from the second step on.
- **Payload.** The can or the nut counts as part of the robot while it is held, that is, while its centre lies within 0.06 m of the end effector and the gripper is closed. A collision of a released object does not count.
- **VLA benchmarks.** On SafeLIBERO and LIBERO-Safety, the robot receives no command for 20 steps after the reset while the objects settle. We exclude a start state when the robot overlaps the obstacle at any of these steps.
- **Isolation experiments.** They count the approaches or paths on which the obstacle collides with the robot without the layer, and they exclude no seed.

### Pick-and-place task

In the pick-and-place scenario, the Panda robot picks a can from a box and places it in a bin. The obstacle is a multi-link human arm, a right arm from the CMU humanoid model. Each obstacle scene runs with ten seeds. The designed set is eight motions of the human arm and a slower copy of each, and each motion targets one link of the robot, such as the wrist or the elbow, or one phase of the task, such as the approach to the can or the carry to the bin. The stress set is 22 static poses of the human arm at two levels of difficulty, eleven at each level, selected by a screen in which the frozen generator completed the task but drove the robot into the obstacle.

### Isolation experiments

The isolation experiments evaluate the safety layer without a generator or a task. The robot holds a fixed pose, which is the reference of the filter, so every motion of the robot comes from the layer and every contact is a failure of the layer.

### Simulated perception

To estimate the obstacle, we simulate a realistic perception pipeline for the multi-link human arm of the pick-and-place scenario. Two simulated depth cameras return a point cloud of the arm, with a depth noise of 0.5 per cent of the distance. They sample the scene on a sparse grid of 40×30 rays or on a dense grid of 160×120 rays. We also simulate a body tracker that returns 11 landmarks of the arm, such as the shoulder, the elbow and the wrist, each at its true position plus a noise calibrated to the published accuracy of the Azure Kinect body tracker against motion capture. The noise grows when the landmark is hidden from both cameras, and the landmarks arrive 200 ms late.

From these measurements we build the obstacle that the filter receives. At each tick, the landmarks divide the point cloud among the segments of the arm, such as the upper arm between the shoulder and the elbow, and we fit one capsule to the points of each segment. Each capsule takes its position from the depth points of the current tick, and the landmarks only decide which points belong to which segment. We estimate the velocity of each capsule from the motion of its segment in the tracked landmarks over the last 0.45 s. The segmentation of the arm is perfect, and we run Diffusion Policy with both grids on both sets of obstacle scenes.

#### Robustness

We add one error at a time to the sparse pipeline on the designed set. We take the types of error from the literature and vary their rates, since no source publishes a rate. For the body tracker, we swap the labels of landmarks, invert the shoulder and the wrist, drop single landmarks, lose the whole skeleton for short periods, and delay the landmarks by up to 800 ms. For the depth cameras, we drop the returns on dark surfaces, and we add flying pixels, which are false returns at the edges of the arm whose depth falls between the arm and the background.
